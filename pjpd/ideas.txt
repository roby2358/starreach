Rebalance combat so it's harder. The designer's target: a sector of 6 enemies should be a nail-biter. To think through before it becomes a task.

Evidence from the first full live game (2026-11-14/15, Claude playing under the captain's orders on starreach.yuwakisa.com): 42 enemies destroyed across a dozen sectors, including fights against 4, 5 and 6, and the hull was never touched. Shields absorbed every hit. The per-action shield recharge and docking undid all damage. Every blast killed in one hit. Enemy shots did about 55-130 from 6-10 cells, about 230 at 4, about 400 at 3, and 674 at 2, so standing off at 4+ made combat nearly free. Enemies often advance instead of firing; on a warp out, all four survivors advanced and none fired. Enemies never pursue between sectors, and the starbase is never threatened, so retreating to dock is always safe. Energy was the only resource that ran down, and docking refilled it. The near-miss and loss-aversion drivers never fired.

What did feel good and should survive a rebalance: the choice between blast (range 6, cost rising with distance, unlimited) and torpedo (any range, 100 energy, 7 of them); stars as cover in both directions, with a refused blocked shot costing nothing; the danger of closing in (the captain's own rule became "blast anything 4 or closer, torpedo the rest"); and the run-down-and-resupply rhythm.

Questions for the designer: What does a nail-biter mean mechanically? E.g. a fresh, fully supplied ship clearing a 6-enemy sector carelessly loses a large share of its hull and sometimes dies, while careful play (cover, range discipline, torpedoes on the far ones) gets through damaged. Should damage persist so that losses accumulate (repair only at a starbase, or a hull that docking doesn't fully restore)? Should escalation be tied to progress (enemies stronger or more numerous as sectors are cleared), per the game-design skill's escalating-commitment driver? Should survivors pursue between sectors, or threaten the starbase, so retreat costs something?

Candidate levers, to change one at a time with a controlled comparison, halving and doubling first: enemy damage and its falloff with distance; shield capacity and the per-action shield recharge; enemies needing more than one blast (enemy hull); how often enemies fire versus advance; enemies firing together versus one at a time; enemy count per sector and how it escalates; what docking restores.

How to do it once it's a task: write the nail-biter as a failable X-criterion in CRITERIA (and the reasoning in DYNAMICS); measure the current numbers with the committed harness (`just cc-model-balance`, extending core/examples/model_balance to simulate a fight against N enemies with a simple careless policy and a simple careful one) before changing anything; bring the proposed numbers and measurements to the user before changing rules; record the numbers as [T] in ONTOLOGY's Numbers table, the decisions and Evidence in RATIONALE, and an ARCHITECTURE_LOG entry.

Time sensitivity: starbases come under siege. On each galactic tick (galaxy time, not combat rounds inside a sector), there is a [T] chance (e.g. SIEGE_CHANCE) that an enemy attack force starts converging on a starbase. Star Central radios the ship: "Starbase at sector (y, x) is under attack and can hold out for N stardates" (a [T] SIEGE_HOLD_TIME, perhaps with seeded variance). If that sector isn't on the galaxy map yet, it is revealed, but only that sector, not its neighbours. Serves: escalating commitment and guardianship (a starbase worth protecting, a clock that forces the ship to leave safety); scarcity of agency (the siege pulls the ship away from its current plan).

Open design questions for the designer:
- What a "galactic tick" is: a fixed stardate interval, or each galaxy_warp? It must not be the in-sector EVENT_INTERVAL.
- What the attack force is: extra enemies spawned in or moving into that sector, and how many?
- What happens at the deadline if the ship hasn't arrived: the starbase is destroyed (no more docking or torpedo resupply there), the sector turns hostile, or the game is lost if the last starbase falls.
- What happens if the ship arrives in time: does the siege end only once the sector is cleared, and does the clock stop on arrival?
- Can there be more than one siege at a time, and should sieges be rarer early or get more likely as the galaxy is cleared?
- How it interacts with ambush on arrival (D-35): a besieged sector is a likely ambush.
- Deterministic from the game's seeded rng; the message arrives as an event the LLM relays in character.

Shape to propose: GameState gets a list of sieges (sector yx, deadline stardate); the stardate-advance path rolls for a new siege on each galactic tick; a starbase whose deadline passes is removed. Docs: an ONTOLOGY rule with pseudocode, constants in the Numbers table, a RATIONALE decision with alternatives, CRITERIA rows and tests, and a log entry.

Fix galactic travel time so that a full battle takes less time than travelling between sectors. Today warp time is nearly free: time = floor(euclid(sectors) * WARP_DISTANCE_SCALE) * WARP_TIME_PER_DISTANCE (core/src/actions.rs), which comes to about 0.1 stardate per sector. Crossing the whole 10x10 galaxy (about 1.27) takes less time than two subwarp cells (SUBWARP_TIME_PER_STEP = 1.0 each), and far less than one long-range scan (LRS_TIME = 3) or one enemy turn interval (EVENT_INTERVAL = 3). Distance between sectors therefore hardly matters for anything time-based.

Goal [D]: warping even to an adjacent sector should take longer than a typical full battle in a sector, so the galaxy feels big, and time spent fighting is small next to time spent getting there. This also lets distance matter to anything time-limited, such as a starbase holding out for a set number of stardates: how far away the ship is should decide whether it can get there.

Steps:
1. Measure first. Add to the committed balance harness (`just cc-model-balance`), or a new recipe, the stardates a typical battle takes (e.g. clearing a sector of 1–6 enemies with blasts and torpedoes under current rules, including LRS_TIME and EVENT_INTERVAL). Today that number isn't known.
2. Set WARP_TIME_PER_DISTANCE (and possibly a fixed per-warp base time, e.g. WARP_TIME_BASE) so that one adjacent warp is longer than the measured typical battle. Halve and double to check the feel.
3. Check the knock-on effects: game length in stardates; whether anything else is time-limited (STARDATE_START, any game deadline, starbase resupply timing); stardate events triggered during a warp (warp_events: does a long warp run many EVENT_INTERVALs of enemy actions in the sector being left or entered? It probably must not); and the stardate display.

Docs: Warp rule and Numbers table in ONTOLOGY; a RATIONALE decision with alternatives (scale time per distance vs. a fixed per-jump cost vs. both) and measured Evidence; CRITERIA and tests (an adjacent warp's time is greater than the battle benchmark constant, or at least warp time is monotone in distance); an ARCHITECTURE_LOG entry; and the galaxy_warp tool description if a player should know.

Game telemetry: record what players actually do, so tuning and design decisions rest on real play rather than the balance model alone.

- A small recipe or report to query it (e.g. per-day games, win rate, blast range histogram) so it feeds RATIONALE Evidence as "Measured by".
- Keep it out of the Rust core's rules: the app layer records Results it already has, so the core stays pure and deterministic.
